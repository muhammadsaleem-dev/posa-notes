Unit 5 · Epistemology & Foundations

Pattern Theory: Origins, Languages & Limits

“Each pattern describes a problem which occurs over and over again in our environment, and then describes the core of the solution to that problem, in such a way that you can use this solution a million times over, without ever doing it the same way twice.” — Christopher Alexander (A Pattern Language, 1977)

Where do patterns come from? The philosophical roots in building architecture, the definition of a pattern language, and the scientific criticisms of software design patterns.

1

5.3 The Origins: Christopher Alexander

From physical architecture in urban planning to object-oriented software engineering.

Design patterns were not invented by computer scientists. They originated with architect Christopher Alexander in books like A Pattern Language (1977) and The Timeless Way of Building (1979).

Alexander observed that master builders over centuries solved recurring structural problems with common harmonious forms: *Light on Two Sides of Every Room*, *A Place to Wait*, *Alcoves*, and *Arcades*.

In 1987, Kent Beck and Ward Cunningham applied Alexander’s architectural concepts to Smalltalk GUI programming. In 1994, the Gang of Four (GoF) popularized the 23 foundational object-oriented design patterns.

5.6.2 Pattern Taxonomies: POSA vs. GoF

Architectural Patterns (POSA)

Fundamental structural organization of whole systems: Layers, Broker, Pipes & Filters, Blackboard, Model-View-Controller.

Design Patterns (GoF)

Medium-scale component refinement: Strategy, Observer, Composite, Command, Abstract Factory, Decorator.

Idioms (Language-Specific)

Low-level implementation idioms: RAII in C++, Double-Checked Locking in Java, Context Managers (with) in Python.

5.6.3 Criticisms & The Limits of Patterns

1. Patterns as "Missing Language Features" (Peter Norvig)

In his famous 1996 analysis, Peter Norvig demonstrated that 16 out of the 23 GoF patterns are either invisible or trivial in first-class dynamic languages (Lisp, Python). For example, Strategy is just passing a first-class function!

2. "Patternitis" (Premature Over-Engineering)

Novice developers often attempt to squeeze as many patterns as possible into a codebase, creating needless indirection and complexity when a simple 5-line function would do.

5.10 Questions for Self-Study

Exam Question What is the distinction between a "Pattern Catalog" and a "Pattern Language"?

A Pattern Catalog (like GoF) is an organized dictionary of independent patterns with alphabetical or topical groupings, but leaves the connections and composition rules largely to the reader.

A Pattern Language is a network of interlocking patterns where the solution of one pattern establishes the context and forces for the next pattern, guiding the developer through a complete design synthesis from macro architecture to micro implementation.
