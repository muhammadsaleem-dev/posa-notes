Unit 10 · Decoupling & Event Handling

Observer & Chain of Responsibility

“Don’t call us, we’ll call you.” — The Hollywood Principle

Decoupling producers of information from consumers through clean publisher-subscriber relationships, and passing requests through a sequential pipeline of handlers.

1

10.3 The Observer Pattern

Publish-Subscribe architecture for state change broadcasting.

GoF Intent: Define a one-to-many dependency between objects so that when one object changes state, all its dependents are notified and updated automatically.

Push vs. Pull Notification Models:

  • Push Model: The Subject broadcasts detailed data bundles to every observer in update(temp, humidity, pressure).
    Pros: Immediate access. Cons: Inflexible if some observers only care about temperature.
  • Pull Model: The Subject simply notifies update(this) or update(), and observers call specific getters to retrieve only what they require.
    Pros: Maximum flexibility and evolution. Cons: Multiple subsequent method calls.
2

The "Lapsed Listener" Memory Leak

⚠️ Critical Production Pitfall:

When an Observer registers with a long-lived Subject, the Subject holds a strong reference in its listener list. If the client forgets to call removeObserver() when discarding the observer, the Garbage Collector cannot reclaim the observer!

Remedies: (1) Strict lifecycle cleanup, or (2) Store observers using WeakReference (or WeakHashMap) so the observer can be collected when no other strong references exist.

10.6 The Chain of Responsibility Pattern

Decoupling request senders from concrete receiver chains.

Intent: Avoid coupling the sender of a request to its receiver by giving more than one object a chance to handle the request. Chain the receiving objects and pass the request along the chain until an object handles it.

10.11 Questions for Self-Study

Exam Drill Compare Chain of Responsibility with the Decorator pattern. How are their structural chains similar, yet their semantic intents completely different?

Structural Similarity:

Both patterns rely on recursively linked object chains implementing a common interface, where an object receives an invocation and delegates to the next node.

Intent Differences:

  • Decorator: Focuses on additive composition. Every decorator executes its own behavior and passes execution down to the core object. The entire chain always executes to augment responsibilities.
  • Chain of Responsibility: Focuses on handling or delegating. A handler in the chain may handle the request and terminate the chain, or choose to pass it forward. Senders do not know which concrete node (if any) will service the request.
